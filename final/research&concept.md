## 1. Research: Inspiration & References

The idea for this project originated from a visit to an exhibition at the Museum of the City of New York:

This Is New York: 100 Years of the City in Art and Pop Culture https://www.mcny.org/exhibition/new-york-100. 

The exhibition space was designed as an immersive room, with screens surrounding the entire space and simultaneously playing film clips shot across different locations in New York.



Rather than presenting films individually, the exhibition emphasized how cinema exists across many places at once, creating a layered and moving image of the city. 

After visiting the exhibition, I began to wonder how many places in New York City have appeared in films. I was curious how dense these locations would become if they were all marked on a map.

I am also personally very interested in films. While trying to visit the staircase location from *Joker*, I noticed that most online resources only described the location using text. Even when images were provided, they were usually fixed, isolated screenshots with little context. This made it difficult to understand how specific scenes related to the broader geography of the city.



While researching film locations online, I found that most existing New York film maps are:

- Static and rarely updated
- Limited to a small number of locations
- Focused on older or canonical films
- Structured as “one film = one location”

This approach does not reflect how films actually unfold spatially. Most films continuously move across neighborhoods, streets, and interiors, and different viewers may care about and want to go to different scenes from the same film. 

I also referenced the Japanese anime pilgrimage website [anitabi.cn/map](http://anitabi.cn/map), which maps specific anime scenes to real-world locations using screenshots. and also https://filmingmap.com/. However, they function mainly as an archive and does not include strong community or discussion features.



After the reseach, this is what I found: 

**there is no continuously updating, community-driven platform that maps specific film scenes—rather than entire films—onto the city, while also allowing discussion and collective moderation.**

---

## 2. Concept

This project is a **community-driven cinematic map of New York City**.

Users can mark specific locations where film scenes take place, rather than assigning a single point to represent an entire movie. Each marker corresponds to:

- **A specific film**
- **A specific scene or moment within that film (So that one movie can have multile markers on the map with different timestamp in the movie)**
- **A precise real-world location**

Clicking a marker leads to a detail page that functions like a forum post. In addition to scene information and a film still, each location includes a discussion section where users can share thoughts, corrections, or related memories.

To support continuous growth and accuracy, the platform includes:

- **All markers and contents are from users**
- **Upvote and downvote mechanisms**
- **A “most popular” view to surface widely supported locations**
- **if too many downvote for a marker, delete it. (prevent wrong or poorly written markers)**

Through these features, the map is able to update, self-correct, and evolve over time as more users contribute.
